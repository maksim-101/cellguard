The one rule for this call

This call is for the bug. It is not for the money.

An engineering manager's currency is the defect. She almost certainly has no authority to issue a goodwill credit — that sits with Customer Relations. If you open the reimbursement request with her, the realistic outcome is that you get recoded from "the reporter who isolated a reproduction Apple's own support missed for four months" into "a customer seeking compensation," and routed back down to Support. You would be trading your single most valuable asset — the engineering channel — for something she cannot grant anyway.

Get the bug number first. That number is what makes the commercial ask land later: it is Apple's own written acknowledgment that the fault is theirs and not yours. Asking for money before it exists throws away the thing that gives the ask its force.

If she raises compensation herself

Let her. Don't refuse it — just don't lead with it, and don't let it become the subject of the call.

Say this
"I do want to talk about how this gets made right — I've been without reliable service for four months and through three handsets. But I'd rather deal with the engineering side properly first, and come back to that once the bug is filed. Can you point me to the right person for it afterwards?"

The boundary — what you claim, and what you do not

✅ Claim this — it is measured and it holds

  • 235 silent failures over 43 monitored days; 23.1% of cellular probes failed silently.
  • The four-arm result: 13.5% → 0%, and the LTE control that changed nothing.
  • Under the failing config, 0 of 111 sliding 23-hour windows were failure-free.
  • 100% of failures are timeouts, 0% are "offline" — the line never detaches.
  • Apple's own "Poor Cell Coverage" battery telemetry lands on the same two clean days.

🚫 Do NOT claim this — you cannot support it, and it is what will cost you her

  • Do not name the failing component. You do not know it is T3245, and you do not know which baseband subsystem is at fault. Offer T3245 as a hypothesis for them to check — the ~05:00 clustering of the long events is a genuine clue — and let their engineers name the part. She will know the modem far better than you do, and one overclaim here converts you from a credible reporter into an amateur with a theory.
  • Do not claim it is a hardware defect in your unit. That framing is dead. Three swaps reproduced it because every iPhone would.
  • Do not say the drops are periodic. They are bursty — retry-with-backoff, not a metronome.

If she says "that's expected in Dual SIM — the other carrier just isn't available here"

Say this
"Then iOS should stop searching — and it already knows how, because that's exactly what manual selection does, and it costs the primary line nothing. A line that has failed to register on every network and every band for four months should not still be sweeping the radio at the expense of the line carrying my data. The retry is unbounded, nothing ever gives up, and the user is never told. I'd accept degraded data. I don't accept a silent black hole the OS reports as healthy."

If Swisscom's analysis comes back "our network is fine"

That helps you, and you should say so out loud. Swisscom's network does not know or care what your second line's network-selection setting is. Congestion cannot switch on and off in lockstep with a menu option on an unregistered second line. Whatever Swisscom reports, the carrier is excluded by the experiment itself — a clean report is confirmation, and an anomalous one is corroboration. You cannot lose this one.

After the call — the commercial track

Your leverage for a Gutschein is not the bug — Apple can always answer "a workaround exists, use manual selection." Your leverage is the cost Apple put you to while failing to diagnose their own defect: four months of degraded service on a premium device, three replacement handsets Apple's own process pushed you through, and an advertised feature (Dual SIM) that cannot be used without silent multi-hour data loss.

Under Swiss sales law a device lacking a warranted property is a Sachmangel, and repeated failed replacement attempts strengthen a claim to Wandelung or Minderung — a price reduction being, in commercial terms, precisely a Gutschein. That is what the Mängelrüge is for, and it is why the legal track must keep running in parallel and must not be dropped just because a workaround now exists.

Set expectations honestly: a goodwill credit at full-iPhone scale is well above what a support or engineering channel normally grants on its own. It usually needs Customer Relations with a consumer-law backdrop behind it. The bug number gets you taken seriously; the Mängelrüge is what gives the number teeth.
